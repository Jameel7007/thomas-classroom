# B2 course blueprint

The B2 path moves the learner from connected B1 communication to independent,
nuanced participation in extended professional, academic, civic, and personal
discussion. The sequence first develops complex time and information structure,
then applies that control to evidence, register, vocabulary precision, and sustained
argument. Each lesson follows the release criteria in
`CONTENT-QUALITY-STANDARD.md`.

| Seq. | Lesson | Independent-use outcome |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Third and mixed conditionals | Reconstruct unreal past causes and connect earlier choices to present consequences. |
| 2 | Past perfect continuous | Explain duration, process, and evidence before a past reference point. |
| 3 | Full passive | Control progressive, perfect, modal, future, and reporting passives to manage focus. |
| 4 | Reported speech | Report questions, commands, requests, and stance through accurate reporting-verb patterns. |
| 5 | Non-defining relative clauses | Add optional context, comment on a whole clause, and use formal preposition patterns. |
| 6 | Modals of past speculation | Rank possible, probable, and impossible past explanations from evidence. |
| 7 | Wish / if only | Distinguish unreal present wishes, past regret, unavailable ability, and desired change. |
| 8 | Causative have / get | Describe arranged services, delegated action, persuasion, and unwanted experience. |
| 9 | Future perfect and continuous | Locate completed results, ongoing activity, and accumulated duration at a future checkpoint. |
| 10 | Cleft sentences | Redirect attention to the person, time, reason, action, or result that matters. |
| 11 | Advanced discourse markers | Signal concession, counterargument, consequence, reformulation, and stance precisely. |
| 12 | Abstract and academic topics | Analyze evidence, assumptions, factors, findings, limitations, and implications. |
| 13 | Politics and global issues | Map institutions, stakeholders, rights, resources, equity, and policy trade-offs neutrally. |
| 14 | Science and innovation | Explain research and development stages and evaluate evidence, scalability, benefits, and risks. |
| 15 | Idioms and fixed expressions | Infer and use common figurative chunks with accurate grammar, tone, and restraint. |
| 16 | Register and formality | Adapt vocabulary, structure, directness, and distance to audience, channel, and purpose. |
| 17 | Nuanced adjectives and intensifiers | Express calibrated degree with natural collocations and carefully distinguished near-synonyms. |
| 18 | Advanced phrasal verbs | Control multiword-verb meaning, object placement, particle patterns, and register shifts. |
| 19 | Connotation and shades of meaning | Interpret and choose words by attitude, strength, agency, semantic preference, and framing. |

## Level transfer task

The end-of-level diagnostic asks the learner to interpret an evidence-based public
argument, follow qualified research and policy listening, control complex grammar
in context, and distinguish neutral description from framing. The learner then
defends a recommendation through follow-up questions and writes a 220–280 word
evidence-based proposal with comparison, counterargument, and evaluation criteria.

B2 is released only when all 19 lessons are `ready` and the automatic diagnostic,
route, asset, audio, interaction, and content-fingerprint gates pass. A passing
result indicates independent B2 performance; it is not an official CEFR certificate.
