## Present perfect continuous: issues found and resolved

- The former lesson had a sound basic form but compressed the topic into nine
  scored interactions. It did not provide enough evidence to separate an
  activity viewpoint from a completed result, current action, or past scene.
- The revision begins with three linked 2:00 p.m. reports: an activity that
  continues, a completed quantity, and a recently stopped activity with visible
  evidence. Learners identify the viewpoint before receiving the rule.
- The earlier wording could imply that present evidence and the continuous form
  prove an activity is still happening. The page now states that the form alone
  does not prove continuation. Explicit context distinguishes `I’m still
  painting` from `I just stopped`.
- Form work now covers positive, negative, yes-or-no, information, and duration
  questions. It keeps `have / has`, `been`, and the `-ing` activity in one
  recoverable chain and revisits `writing, running`, and `lying` spelling.
- Five present connections are separated: activity continuing now, repetition
  in an unfinished period, recent evidence, a temporary current situation, and
  a negative recent pattern.
- Present continuous, past continuous, present perfect continuous, and present
  perfect simple are organized as four time lenses. The learner chooses a
  viewpoint instead of searching for a nearby signal word.
- The simple-continuous contrast now foregrounds process or duration versus
  completed result or quantity. It also states that continuous `I’ve been
  reading` does not promise completion and that a completed achievement such as
  `has finished` should not be forced into a continuous form.
- `For`, `since`, complete spans such as `all morning`, and broad recent periods
  with `lately / recently` are tied to the exact time information each supplies.
- State verbs are no longer presented as a flat list of permanently prohibited
  words. The lesson contrasts state and activity meanings in `think` and `have`,
  and explains why `live, work`, and `study` may allow both perfect viewpoints
  in some contexts.
- Spoken guidance covers contractions, common U.S. /bɪn/, final consonants in
  `I’ve / we’ve`, weak `have you`, information stress, and contrastive repair.
  It avoids the false claim that one fixed word must always receive stress.
- A six-item tutor-read check distinguishes continuing activity, completed
  quantity, repeated attempts, recent evidence, a duration question, and a
  negative recent pattern before the transcript opens.
- The reading makes the continuation boundary explicit. Maintenance has been
  mopping, but the next sentence states that the activity stopped one minute
  ago. The wet floor remains evidence, not proof of current action.
- All 49 scored decisions contain a stated timeline, requested information job,
  duration or starting-point cue, result or quantity, state or activity meaning,
  reading fact, listening fact, or exact repair context. Every item has a
  contextual first hint, exact fix, and concept-specific correct feedback.
- The former production task requested real projects, habits, and emotional
  results. Fictional Harbor forum cards now supply equivalent evidence. Real
  work, schedules, sleep, health, relationships, and other private details stay
  optional.
- The final 60-to-75-second status briefing requires an ongoing duration,
  repeated attempt, recently stopped activity with evidence, completed result,
  continuing state, follow-up question, and explanation of each aspect choice.
  Next-day retrieval revisits a state verb, a starting point, and recent
  evidence without falsely forcing continuation.

## Crime & society: issues found and resolved

- The former lesson had a sound neutral-reporting principle but compressed
  offense meanings, legal roles, evidence, prevention, listening, and production
  into thirteen scored decisions. The revision now contains 63 contextual
  decisions across retrieval, offense classification, verb relationships,
  legal stages, allegation grammar, evidence limits, reported-crime data,
  listening, reading, building, repair, production, and delayed retrieval.
- One fictional Northside Community Hub incident now anchors the lesson. The
  employee observation, inventory, camera, alarm, closing checklist, witness
  account, police update, newsletter correction, and response pilot remain
  separate sources with explicit limits.
- Theft, robbery, burglary, shoplifting, vandalism, fraud, identity theft, and
  cybercrime are distinguished by the facts supplied in each item. The lesson
  states that exact legal definitions and categories vary by jurisdiction and
  tells learners to describe an observed act when the official category is not
  known.
- `Steal + thing`, `steal + thing + from + person/place`, `rob + person/place`,
  `break into + building/vehicle/account`, and `commit + crime/offense/fraud`
  now preserve the thing taken, source, affected participant, entry, and act.
  `Burglarize` is identified as standard U.S. English, while `break into` is
  prioritized for natural conversation.
- Crime is shown as a general uncountable problem or a countable act. Evidence
  remains uncountable and is counted through pieces or items rather than
  `evidences`.
- Victim, witness, suspect, defendant, and convicted offender are tied to the
  role each record establishes. The page does not substitute `criminal` or
  `offender` for `suspect` before responsibility is established.
- Investigation, arrest, charge, trial or hearing, verdict, and sentence are
  separated. Arrest does not establish guilt; charge does not equal
  conviction; acquittal or a not-guilty verdict means no conviction was
  obtained rather than supplying a universal account of every fact.
- `Alleged + noun`, `allegedly + verb`, `accuse someone of`, `charge someone
  with`, `convict someone of`, and `sentence someone to` now encode distinct
  claim and process relationships. Controlled items supply the exact stage
  needed for one defensible frame.
- Evidence can show an observable detail, support a claim, suggest a
  possibility, contradict an account, or remain inconclusive. Camera,
  witness, tool-mark, access-log, and anonymous-post items each preserve what
  the evidence does and does not establish.
- The lesson rejects using deductive `must` to declare legal guilt from a few
  clues. It also distinguishes unverified from automatically true or false.
- Reported crime is defined as incidents recorded by the stated system rather
  than every incident that occurred. Reporting access, recording rules,
  enforcement priorities, and real incident changes remain possible
  explanations when a reported count changes.
- Community response includes practical support, reduced opportunity, fair
  process, rehabilitation, access, cost, timeframe, and measurement. Security
  advice is not used to transfer responsibility for an offense to a victim.
- U.S. spoken guidance covers the final /ft/ cluster in `theft`, three-syllable
  `burglary`, stress in `evidence`, noun-verb stress shifts in `SUSpect` and
  `susPECT` and `CONvict` and `conVICT`, linking in `charged with`, and
  contrastive stress across arrest and conviction.
- The five-item tutor-read check tests the theft cluster, noun-verb stress,
  charged-with meaning, an access-record identity limit, and the
  arrest-versus-conviction contrast before written support is used.
- The expanded Northside file supplies every time, inventory total, visible
  action, missing detail, witness limit, official stage, public correction,
  pilot measure, and causal boundary needed for five defensible reading
  answers.
- All 63 scored decisions have an incident fact, participant, legal stage,
  source, evidence limit, reporting condition, listening line, reading record,
  or exact repair target. Every item has a contextual first hint, exact fix,
  and concept-specific correct feedback.
- Private-safe practice never requires a real victimization, accusation,
  arrest, court case, immigration matter, address, financial loss, identity
  document, workplace incident, family conflict, police contact, political
  belief, or traumatic event. The page states that it teaches neutral English,
  not legal advice, and never asks the learner to perform a criminal role from
  personal experience.
- Final production requires the reported act, affected property, sources,
  evidence, missing identity, corrected headline, current legal stage, one
  accurate verb frame, fair response, access concern, six-month measure, and a
  skeptical-question response. Delayed retrieval revisits offense meaning,
  verb participants, legal stage, evidence limits, and evaluable response.

## Collocations & word families: issues found and resolved

- The former lesson had useful core examples but compressed collocation types,
  grammatical slots, word-family meaning, register, pronunciation, and
  production into thirteen scored decisions. The revision now contains 63
  contextual decisions across retrieval, partnership type, verb-noun networks,
  modifiers, grammatical jobs, family meaning, register, listening, reading,
  building, repair, production, and delayed retrieval.
- The former pronunciation note incorrectly suggested first-syllable stress in
  `decide`. The revision gives natural U.S. `deCIDE` /dɪˈsaɪd/ and contrasts it
  accurately with `deCIsion` /dɪˈsɪʒən/ and `deCIsive` /dɪˈsaɪsɪv/.
- A collocation is defined as a conventional retrievable partnership rather
  than a universal grammar rule. Naturalness, meaning, frequency, region, and
  register can vary, and deliberate creative combinations remain possible.
- A word family is treated as a meaning network whose members have different
  grammatical jobs and sometimes distinct meanings. Learners are warned
  against attaching an imagined suffix without checking the real form.
- Verb-noun, adjective-noun, adverb-adjective, adverb-verb, word-preposition,
  and family-member relationships are identified through complete frames.
  Each controlled item supplies enough syntax and meaning to establish one
  intended decision.
- `Make`, `do` or `conduct`, `take`, `reach` or `come to`, `meet`, `raise`, and
  `gain` are organized around project situations rather than an unranked list.
  The page also shows how several verbs can partner with `decision` while
  expressing different stages.
- Modifier practice distinguishes `heavy traffic`, `strong evidence`, `deeply
  concerned`, `highly effective`, `widely available`, and `seriously consider`
  through semantic preference. `Very` remains a legitimate natural choice
  rather than being presented as automatically inferior.
- Determiners, possessives, tense markers, linking verbs, noun positions,
  action verbs, and prepositions provide grammatical-slot evidence. The page
  also states that position alone is not automatic because meaning still
  decides between possible complements.
- Decide, apply, employ, produce, succeed, and rely families now include action,
  person, process, result, description, and manner distinctions. `Employer`
  and `employee`, `production` and `productivity`, `application` and
  `applicant`, and `success` and `successful` remain meaningfully separate.
- Suffixes such as `-tion`, `-ment`, `-ity`, `-ive`, `-al`, `-ful`, and `-ly`
  are described as clues, not mechanical guarantees. `Friendly`, `lively`,
  `likely`, and `lonely` demonstrate adjective forms ending in `-ly`.
- Legitimate U.S. alternatives remain visible: `do research` is neutral while
  `conduct research` is more formal; `strongly recommend` and `highly
  recommend` are both natural; `reach an agreement` and `come to an agreement`
  are both standard; `big problem` and `major issue`, and `totally agree` and
  `strongly agree`, differ partly by setting and tone.
- U.S. spoken guidance covers stress and sound changes in the decide, apply,
  and photograph families; noun `PROgress` versus verb `proGRESS`; and linking
  in `make_an_effort`, `reach_an_agreement`, and `responsible_for`.
- The five-item tutor-read check tests noun-verb progress stress, decide-family
  sound changes, application and successful jobs, two valid recommendation
  patterns, and linked agreement before written support is used.
- The Westbridge Skills Exchange file supplies the four-week survey, 80-person
  sample, 46 evening-workshop requests, 29 transportation reports, project
  achievements, grant result, attendance, regular-participant feedback,
  evidence limits, access concerns, and next measures needed for five
  defensible reading answers.
- Attendance and feedback are kept positive without being used to claim
  unmeasured employment effects or causation. The final production also
  requires the learner to preserve that evidence limit.
- Two builders combine collocation retrieval with family jobs. Three repairs
  target `did progress`, adverb-before-noun `successfully application`, and
  adjective-after-action `explained clear` with precise hints, fixes, and
  explanations.
- The vocabulary-record model stores a core chunk, meaning and context,
  natural alternative, family, sound, contrast, and new sentence. It teaches a
  maintainable retrieval practice rather than encouraging large unranked lists.
- All 63 scored decisions have a project fact, lexical relationship,
  grammatical slot, family meaning, register condition, spoken line, reading
  record, or exact repair target. Every item has a contextual first hint,
  exact fix, and concept-specific correct feedback.
- Private-safe practice never requires a real application, rejection,
  unemployment, performance review, employer, salary, failed deadline,
  academic result, immigration application, health information, financial
  difficulty, or private project. Westbridge and tutor-supplied fictional
  contexts remain available throughout.
- Final production requires research, four verb-noun partnerships, modifiers,
  three family transformations, a preposition partnership, a register
  alternative, evidence limits, a recommendation, correction reuse, and
  accurate stress. Delayed retrieval revisits partnership, slot, preposition,
  register, and stress.

## Discourse linkers: issues found and resolved

- The former lesson had an accurate compact overview but compressed a broad
  discourse outcome into fourteen scored decisions. The revision now contains
  63 contextual decisions across retrieval, noticing, logical function,
  structure, cause and result, concession, placement, register, logical audit,
  listening, reading, building, repair, production, and delayed retrieval.
- Linker choice now begins with the relationship between ideas rather than a
  memorized list. Addition, contrast, concession, reason, result, example or
  clarification, sequence, and conclusion each have a communicative job and a
  complete contextual model.
- The lesson explicitly states that a linker cannot create missing logic or
  replace evidence. Learners diagnose unsupported inference, empty repetition,
  irrelevant examples, supported results, and accurate contrasts before
  polishing connector vocabulary.
- Grammatical form distinguishes coordinating conjunctions, dependent-clause
  linkers, preposition phrases, and sentence connectors. `Because` and
  `although` take clauses; `because of` and `despite` take noun phrases or
  appropriate `-ing` forms; `but` and `so` join clauses; `however` and
  `therefore` receive dependable sentence-connector punctuation.
- Double frames are corrected without inventing a universal ban: standard
  edited English uses either `because` or `so`, and either `although` or `but`,
  in the supplied cause-result and concession pairs. `Even though` is presented
  as a potentially stronger concession, while `despite` and `in spite of`
  retain their phrase structure.
- Addition and contrast practice covers natural `also` placement before a main
  verb, after `be`, and after the first auxiliary; sentence-final `too`; formal
  `moreover`; and conversational final `though`. Register is treated as a
  contextual preference, not a false grammatical hierarchy.
- `For example` is separated from `such as` through sentence versus noun-phrase
  structure. `Overall` and `in conclusion` close a complete discussion rather
  than pretending to express a physical or logical consequence.
- The lesson preserves the boundary between a valid conclusion and an
  overclaim. A start date cannot support a permanent employment result,
  restating `inexpensive` as `does not cost much` adds no new support, and a
  library closing time cannot illustrate a transport-service claim.
- U.S. spoken guidance treats opening sentence connectors as short intonation
  groups, preserves information stress in the main clause, shows reduced
  conversational `and` toward /ən/, gives a natural reduced form of `because`,
  and uses prominent `however` and final `though` to make contrast audible.
- The five-item tutor-read check tests opening connector chunking, reduced
  conjunctions, corrective stress, final `though`, and cause-result direction
  before written support is used.
- The fictional Riverbend file supplies a 9:30 workshop ending, 9:20 last direct
  bus, 120 of 190 survey responses, 47 reported early departures, a 12-week
  trial, two proposed departure times, a $7,200 quote, accessibility
  requirements, evidence limits, and defined trial measures.
- The Riverbend argument preserves both value and uncertainty. The optional
  self-reports identify a timing concern but do not establish future shuttle
  use, attendance improvement, employment outcomes, or effects for all
  registered learners.
- Five reading decisions require the learner to reconstruct the timing
  contrast, the limited job of `therefore`, the accessibility addition, the
  survey boundary, and the continue-revise-stop recommendation.
- Two builders create a concession that preserves limited evidence and a
  measured conclusion. Three repairs target double contrast, `because of`
  before a clause, and a comma splice with `however` using contextual hints,
  exact fixes, and explanations.
- All 63 scored decisions have a stated logical relationship, grammatical
  frame, sentence position, channel, register, evidence record, spoken line,
  reading fact, or precise repair target. Every item has a contextual first
  hint, exact fix, and concept-specific correct feedback.
- Private-safe practice never requires a real employer, commute, disability,
  financial problem, immigration matter, school record, family conflict, or
  unsafe travel experience. Riverbend and tutor-created fictional proposals
  remain available throughout.
- Final production requires two reasons, a distinct addition, relevant example,
  option contrast, real concession, supported result, measured recommendation,
  three different linker structures, audible chunking, and live reformulation
  after a partner challenges one weak connection.
- Delayed retrieval revisits function-first choice, clause and phrase form,
  `however` punctuation, addition register, and an evidence-bound conclusion.

## Money & spending: issues found and resolved

- The former lesson had a useful transaction direction but compressed a broad,
  sensitive topic into thirteen scored decisions. The revision now contains 59
  constrained decisions across inflows, outflows, countability, transaction
  roles, price, total cost, affordability, value, U.S. checkout language,
  planning, speech, listening, reading, building, repair, and retrieval.
- The opening Harbor quote gives a $480 listed price, $38.40 sales tax, $20
  delivery fee, $538.40 pay-in-full total, $550 equipment limit, and twelve
  $47 installments totaling $564. Learners identify each amount’s job before
  receiving the vocabulary explanation.
- Salary, hourly wage, income, revenue, expense, and profit are separated by
  source and calculation. Revenue is not treated as profit or employee pay,
  and ordinary `income` remains uncountable without incorrectly banning
  comparative or source-based plural `incomes`.
- The earlier statement that `money`, `cost`, and `worth` were all uncountable
  was inaccurate. The revision keeps `money`, `cash`, and money-related
  `change` uncountable; makes dollars, coins, bills, fees, and expenses
  countable; contrasts singular `total cost` with plural `additional costs`;
  and distinguishes plural `savings` from `a saving of $20`.
- `Cost`, `charge`, `pay`, and `spend` now assign the item, provider, buyer,
  amount, and purpose explicitly. Controlled practice stores `something costs
  an amount`, `charge someone an amount for something`, `pay someone an amount
  or pay an amount for something`, and `spend an amount on something`.
- Borrowing language follows the complete transfer: the receiver `borrows from`
  the giver, the giver `lends to` the receiver or `lends the receiver` the item,
  the unpaid party `owes` the person due the amount, and `pay back` closes the
  temporary transfer. The page does not imply that every amount owed is a loan.
- Listed price, required added charge, total cost, affordability, and value are
  separate claims. Affordability depends on stated resources and priorities;
  value also requires evidence about benefit, quality, useful life,
  suitability, and alternatives.
- `Cheap`, `inexpensive`, and `expensive` no longer function as automatic
  quality judgments. Low price does not prove good value, and a high relative
  price does not by itself prove waste.
- Common U.S. transaction language now includes restaurant `check`, amount-due
  `bill`, payment `receipt`, fee, sales tax, deposit, refund, warranty, service
  charge, and tip. The lesson explicitly asks learners to use the stated place,
  policy, and agreement because tax, deposit, refund, warranty, and service
  practices vary.
- Budget, tracking spending, saving up, cutting back, loan, principal, interest,
  installment, and debt are defined by their language function. The page

## Reported speech: issues found and resolved

- The former lesson had a sound viewpoint principle but compressed the topic
  into twelve scored decisions. Its retrieval prompt for an object pronoun
  explicitly allowed several answers. The revision now supplies 58 constrained
  decisions, and every person, time, place, and reporting purpose is stated.
- The lesson begins with two complete speaking situations: Mina speaks to Alex
  at Harbor Station on Monday morning, and Alex reports to Jordan from
  Riverside Office later that day. Learners map reference before changing form.
- Exact direct quotation is separated from paraphrased reported meaning.
  Quotation marks are used only when the wording is known; a paraphrase does
  not pretend to be a transcript.
- `Said + clause`, `told + listener + clause`, and `said to + person` receive
  separate form and meaning practice. Optional conversational `that` is
  acknowledged without licensing `said me` or active `told that` without a
  listener.
- Common backshifts now cover Present Simple, Present Continuous, Present
  Perfect, Past Simple, `can`, and `will`. Past Perfect, `could`, and `would`
  are shown as forms that normally do not shift further.
- Backshift is no longer presented as an automatic replacement rule. Present
  reporting retains a present form; still-current information may deliberately
  remain current; and informal Past Simple may remain when past event order is
  clear. Past Perfect marks the earlier layer more explicitly. The lesson does
  not reject a grammatical alternative to manufacture one answer.
- Pronouns and possessives follow identified people rather than a memorized
  conversion table. Original Mina `I/my` becomes Alex’s `she/her`, while
  original listener Alex `you/your` becomes `I/me/my` in Alex’s own report.
- Time, place, and demonstrative changes are also reference-based. `Today`,
  `tomorrow`, `here`, and `this` remain possible when the new situation still
  supports them; the controlled Monday office context explicitly requires
  `that day`, `the next day`, `there`, and `that`.
- Reporting accuracy now includes speech-act stance. Neutral `said` is kept
  separate from `promised`, which adds commitment, and `claimed`, which can
  suggest distance or doubt. Learners do not invent those meanings.
- The scope is explicitly statements. The page warns learners not to force the
  statement pattern onto reported questions or commands before the expanded
  B2 system.
- U.S. spoken guidance covers /sɛd/, /toʊld/, weak or omitted /ðət/, linked
  `said she` and `told me`, message stress, and the base-versus-participle
  evidence that resolves contracted `’d` as `would` or `had`.
- A five-item tutor-read check tests listener framing, a backshifted temporary
  state, an explicitly earlier cancellation, a reported future, and
  `would/had` contraction resolution before the transcript opens.
- The reading note makes its no-backshift decision explicit: Alex keeps

## Defining relative clauses: issues found and resolved

- The former lesson had an accurate identifying-clause principle but compressed
  the topic into twelve scored decisions. The revision now contains 58 scored
  decisions across reference tracing, clause anatomy, relative-word choice,
  omission, agreement, boundaries, information structure, register, listening,
  reading, building, repair, production, and delayed retrieval.
- The lesson begins with a complete workshop shortlist. Several trainers,
  projectors, rooms, and organizations are possible, so each relative clause
  performs a real identifying job rather than decorating an isolated example.
- Relative clauses are built from two ideas that share a referent. The learner
  locates the noun, the subject inside the clause, and the one role carried by
  the relative word or understood gap before selecting a form.
- Subject and object relatives are no longer reduced to a memorized omission
  rule. A finite verb directly after the relative marker signals a subject gap;
  a separate subject before the verb signals an object gap. Only the object
  relative may normally be omitted in the practiced patterns.
- `Who`, `which`, and `that` receive accurate reference guidance. The lesson
  states that defining `which` is grammatical for things even though some U.S.
  editorial styles prefer `that`, so it does not manufacture a false grammar
  error from a style preference.
- Singular and plural agreement follows the noun being identified: `the tutor
  who works` contrasts with `the tutors who work`. The relative marker does not
  erase the head noun’s number.
- `Whose + noun` expresses possession or a natural association with a person or
  organization. `Where` carries the place relationship itself, while `that ...
  in` keeps the preposition with the verb. The lesson rejects the redundant
  combination `where ... in` in the controlled context.
- `What` is separated from post-noun relative markers. It can mean roughly `the
  thing that` when no noun is expressed, but it does not follow an existing
  noun in standard `the point that Nia made`.
- Defining information remains integrated with its noun and normally has no
  comma break. A trained-volunteer example makes the subset meaning explicit,
  then previews the different all-member meaning of a non-defining clause
  without trying to teach the full B2 system early.
- Everyday preposition stranding, such as `the coordinator I spoke to`, is
  contrasted with formal fronted patterns such as `the coordinator to whom I
  spoke`. The lesson does not permit `that` after a fronted preposition.
- U.S. spoken guidance treats the noun and defining clause as one rhythm group.
  It covers unstressed relative words, weak /ðət/, linked zero object relatives,
  content-word focus, and the absence of a strong comma-like pause.
- A five-item tutor-read check tests a required subject relative, zero object
  relative, weak `that`, `whose` association, and conversational-to-formal
  preposition contrast before its transcript opens.
- Every scored decision has an identified referent, explicit clause role,
  agreement cue, requested register, stated reading or listening fact, or exact
  repair target. Each has a contextual first hint, exact fix, and
  concept-specific correct feedback.
- The fictional workshop removes pressure to reveal real coworkers, clients,
  family members, employers, locations, schedules, accessibility needs, or
  safety records. The final 75-to-90-second shortlist requires four reference
  types, a required subject relative, zero object relative, agreement, and a
  spoken information-flow explanation. Delayed retrieval revisits omission,
  place patterns, and repeated-object repair.

## Modals of deduction: issues found and resolved

- The former lesson had a sound certainty distinction but compressed the topic
  into twelve scored decisions. The revision now contains 58 scored decisions
  across observation, inference strength, form, function, evidence quality,
  present viewpoint, questions, listening, reading, building, repair,
  production, and delayed retrieval.
- Several former examples did not make the keyed strength defensible. A car
  outside did not prove that its owner could not be home, and white ground did
  not prove that snow was currently falling. The revision uses a fully stated
  2:10 p.m. North Hall case with recent check-in, labeled equipment, sound,
  room checks, locations, and explicit timing.
- Observation and inference are separated before any modal is selected. The
  reception record and room check are observations; `must`, `might`, `could`,
  and `can’t` communicate the speaker’s reasoning stance toward those facts.
- `Must` is a strong positive inference, not proof. The lesson rejects fixed
  modal percentages and states that evidence quality, live alternatives, and
  speaker judgment affect the choice. A responsible speaker verifies and
  revises when needed.
- `Might` and `could` keep one or more explanations possible. `Might not`
  presents a possible negative and remains weaker than `can’t`, which requires
  a reliable contradiction in the controlled cases. One unanswered call is no
  longer treated as decisive negative evidence.
- Form work covers `modal + be` for states and locations, `modal + base verb`
  for knowledge, possession, or regular facts, and `modal + be + -ing` for an
  activity inferred to be in progress. No modal takes `to` or third-person
  agreement.
- `May` is acknowledged as a standard possibility modal rather than falsely
  marked wrong. Past conclusions with `modal + have + participle` are explicitly
  reserved for the later past-speculation system.
- Deduction is distinguished from obligation, ability, general possibility,
  and prohibition through complete contexts. `Visitors must sign in`, `Rina
  can use the mixer`, `The hall can get noisy`, and evidence-based conclusions
  no longer share an unexplained label.
- The negative boundary is accurate for U.S. English. `Can’t` is the clearest
  core strong negative deduction; `mustn’t` commonly expresses prohibition.
  Full `must not` can sometimes express inference but may be ambiguous, so the
  lesson does not declare every natural inferential use impossible.
- Evidence quality is now an explicit language goal. Missing information is not
  automatically a contradiction, and identity, appearance, accent,
  nationality, age, gender, or disability is not accepted as evidence for a
  conclusion about a person.
- Question work covers natural `Could Rina be...?` and conversational `Do you
  think Rina might be...?`. It warns that `Must Rina be...?` can invite a
  necessity reading instead of clearly asking about likelihood.
- U.S. spoken guidance covers modal-to-`be` linking, light or unreleased final
  /t/, recoverable negative `can’t`, information stress, and contrastive repair.
  It avoids claiming that the same word always receives stress.
- A five-item tutor-read check separates an observation from a strong positive
  inference, two live possibilities, a contradiction-based negative inference,
  and contrastive correction of `might` versus `must` before the transcript
  opens.
- Every scored decision has a named observation, timeline, current state or
  activity, remaining alternative, contradiction, requested function,
  listening or reading fact, or exact repair target. Each has a contextual
  first hint, exact fix, and concept-specific correct feedback.
- The North Hall scenario removes pressure to investigate real people,
  workplaces, health, relationships, identity, locations, or private behavior.
  The final 75-to-90-second briefing requires observations, two possibilities,
  a possible negative, strong positive and negative conclusions, state and
  activity form, a natural question, verification, and delayed retrieval.

- The former vocabulary lesson offered a useful initial language bank but
  compressed the topic into thirteen scored decisions. The revision now
  contains 58 scored decisions across lexical scale, employment relationships,
  responsibilities, applications, professional evidence, career changes,
  listening, reading, building, repair, production, and delayed retrieval.
- The opening professional introduction separates role, organization, team,
  recurring duties, promotion path, action, and measurable result. Learners do
  not have to treat every professional detail as a job title or achievement.

  or employment, and `career` as development across roles and years. The page
  preserves countable `work` in other meanings, such as a work of art, without
  allowing `many works` for ordinary tasks.
- The British-leaning example `changed career` was replaced with natural U.S.
  `changed careers` and `changed career paths`.

  client work are distinguished by what they foreground while acknowledging
  their common overlap instead of forcing mutually exclusive categories.
- Hours, contract period, and work location are treated as separate dimensions.
  A role can therefore be full-time, temporary, and hybrid at the same time.
- Responsibility language is stored as complete relationships: `work as`,
  `work for`, `work in`, `be responsible for`, `report to`, `collaborate with`,
  `meet a deadline`, and `take responsibility for`.

  managing deadlines. `Report to` identifies an organizational relationship;
  it is not interpreted as the action of writing a report.

  `Job advertisement` remains clear, while `vacancy` is described accurately

  organization or program. Applicant and candidate are presented as
  overlapping labels with different common selection-stage emphasis.
- The job-posting hierarchy separates responsibilities, required
  qualifications, preferred qualifications, work arrangement, and application
  instructions. A preferred software system is not silently converted into an
  essential requirement.

  different evidence question. `Three years of experience` remains
  uncountable, while a countable event such as `a useful experience` is

  relationships, and supported achievement.

## Question tags: issues found and resolved

- The former lesson had the correct opposite-polarity core but compressed the
  topic into twelve scored decisions. The revision now contains 58 scored

  special patterns, response logic, social use, listening, reading, building,
  repair, production, and delayed retrieval.

  polarity, or question behavior. `Be`, perfect `have`, modals, and future
  `will` are echoed; lexical Present and Past Simple supply `do`, `does`, or

  the complement to resolve `he’s`: a noun phrase identifies `he is`, while a
  participle identifies Present Perfect `he has`.
- Contemporary U.S. lexical possession now receives explicit `do`-support:
  `You have the room key, don’t you?` Perfect `have` remains `You’ve checked
  the room, haven’t you?` The page acknowledges the standard `haven’t you?`
  possessive pattern in British and other varieties rather than declaring it
  universally wrong.

  ordinary pronouns, singular `they` after `everyone, someone, no one`, and
  `nobody`, `it` after `this, that`, and `nothing`, plural `they`, and preserved
  existential `there`.

  hardly, rarely, seldom`, and `scarcely` take positive tags even without a
  visible `not`.

  confirmation patterns.

  confirms `You haven’t sent it`; `Yes, I have` corrects that negative

  voice as a common open-check signal, but neither receives a universal fixed
  meaning. Variety, relationship, emotion, and discourse can change the social
  effect.

  routine, challenging, or coercive. When a listener must have genuine freedom
  to disagree, the speaker rebuilds the assumption as a neutral full question.
- U.S. spoken guidance treats the tag as an intonation unit and covers linked

  operator, polarity, and subject reference; contrastive stress remains

## Environment & nature: issues found and resolved

- The former lesson had a useful cause-effect-response frame but compressed a
  large B1 topic into thirteen scored decisions. The revision now contains 58
  contextualized decisions across time scale, physical systems, ecological
  relationships, evidence, response types, listening, reading, building,
  repair, production, and delayed retrieval.
- The opening fictional river update separates a direct water-test finding,
  one contributing discharge source, a mechanism involving plant growth and
  oxygen, a possible effect on fish, a proposed treatment response, and a later
  evidence check. A proposal is not presented as a proven solution.
- Weather now names short-term atmospheric conditions; climate names patterns
  and variation over a long period. One hot or cool afternoon is not treated as
  enough evidence to establish or reject a climate trend.
- Pollution, waste, and litter have distinct relationships. Pollution is
  harmful contamination, waste is discarded material, and litter is material
  left in the wrong public place. The page states when waste can become
  pollution instead of treating the words as synonyms.
- An emission is introduced as something released into the air. Greenhouse
  gases are grouped by their heat-trapping effect. Fossil-fuel use can produce
  greenhouse-gas emissions and substances that contribute to local air
  pollution, but those categories are not collapsed into one.
- Renewable energy is defined through a naturally replenished source without
  being described as impact-free. Project evaluation can still consider land,
  materials, cost, reliability, nearby communities, and habitat effects.
- Habitat, species, wildlife, ecosystem, and biodiversity are organized by the
  relationship each noun encodes. The same singular and plural form of
  `species` and the normally uncountable uses of `wildlife` and `biodiversity`
  receive contextual practice.
- Conservation protects an existing species, habitat, or resource;
  restoration helps a damaged ecosystem recover. Deforestation and habitat
  loss are connected cautiously, with place, scale, species, and later land
  use left available as relevant variables.
- Evidence language now distinguishes direct findings from interpretation and
  causal claims. `Recorded`, `found`, and `show` report supplied observations;
  `may`, `might`, `could`, and `suggests` calibrate uncertainty;
  `contributes to` identifies one factor among several.
- `Results from` points from an effect back to a cause; `results in` points
  from a cause forward to an effect. `Affect` takes a direct object. The lesson
  explicitly rejects upgrading `is associated with` to `causes` without
  stronger evidence.
- Mitigation, adaptation, conservation, restoration, and waste prevention are
  classified by primary purpose. Concrete contexts make each controlled
  answer defensible while the page acknowledges that a real project can serve
  more than one purpose.
- Individual and system-level action are not framed as moral opposites. The
  lesson asks who has the power to change infrastructure, access, rules, or
  product design, and who benefits, pays, maintains, or bears a new risk.
- U.S. spoken guidance gives accurate stress and phonemic support for
  `climate`, `habitat`, `pollution`, `renewable`, `emissions`, and two-syllable
  `species`. Linking in `result in` and `protect our habitat` supports
  intelligibility without requiring imitation of one accent.
- A five-item tutor-read check tests the weather-climate contrast, pollution
  stress, one-factor causal language, adaptation purpose, and habitat-species
  meaning before the transcript is used as support.
- The fictional Arroyo City heat brief limits its observation to a stated
  six-week comparison and six stops. It supplies the proposal,
  mechanism, costs, water and maturity limits, two-summer evaluation period,
  and four outcome measures needed for every reading answer.
- All 58 scored decisions now have a stated time scale, measured substance,
  ecological relationship, evidence strength, causal direction, response
  purpose, trade-off, listening line, reading fact, or exact repair target.
  Every decision has a contextual first hint, exact fix, and concept-specific
  correct feedback.
- The final council briefing never requires a real address, utility bill,
  health condition, immigration status, workplace practice, political view,
  financial situation, or conflict. Fictional evidence supports the complete
  production route.
- Final production requires one direct observation, supported cause and
  effect, cautious interpretation, classified response, benefit, trade-off,
  measurable next step, and review-panel answer. Delayed retrieval revisits
  time scale, habitat, evidence calibration, mitigation versus adaptation, and
  an evaluable recommendation.

## Media & news: issues found and resolved

- The former lesson offered a strong basic verification principle but
  compressed the topic into thirteen scored decisions. The revision now
  contains 58 contextualized decisions across product purpose, claim chains,
  evidence status, attribution, verification, speech, listening, reading,
  building, repair, production, and delayed retrieval.
- The opening Metrovale case presents a headline’s absolute promise, a city
  office press release, a simulation estimate, the missing measured outcome, a
  reporter’s verification request, and a reader’s opinion. Learners identify
  each move before receiving terminology.
- Headline, news report, analysis, editorial or opinion piece, and press release
  are distinguished by their usual communication purpose and limitation.
  Sponsored content is labeled as paid promotion. The page states that a
  content label helps interpretation but does not settle truth by itself.
- Publisher, author or reporter, source, claim, evidence, context, and
  independent confirmation now occupy separate roles. Several sites copying
  one release or wire item do not count as several independent confirmations.
- Primary source is defined as proximity to the original event, document,
  measurement, or statement. It is not treated as a synonym for neutral,
  complete, or automatically correct. Learners ask what exact question each
  source can answer.
- A factual claim is checkable but is not automatically a fact before checking.
  `Supported fact` is used only when the exercise supplies adequate reliable
  records. Opinion, inference, and current verification status remain separate.
- `Unverified` means that checking is insufficient, not that a claim is
  automatically true or false. This protects learners from replacing automatic
  trust with automatic disbelief.
- Bias is taught as sustained selection, emphasis, or language favoring a
  viewpoint. Bias does not make every detail false, and a neutral tone does not
  prove accuracy. Important claims still require individual evaluation.
- Attribution practice separates `according to + source noun`, `source +
  says`, `said + clause`, and `told + person + clause`. Direct quotation is
  reserved for supplied exact words; paraphrase reports meaning without false
  quotation marks.
- Neutral `said` and `reports` remain separate from distancing `claims` and
  evaluative `admits`. Learners do not invent doubt, reluctance, or another
  stance that the source situation does not supply.
- An unnamed source is distinguished from an unidentified online post. A
  professional reporting team may know and protect the source’s identity while
  explaining relevant access and seeking corroboration. Readers’ reduced
  ability to assess the source directly remains a stated limitation.
- The verification route now checks the full item, author, publisher, date,
  updates, corrections, content label, original quotation or dataset, method,
  sample, baseline, denominator, timeframe, comparison, and genuinely separate
  coverage.
- Percentage language distinguishes a ten-percentage-point rise from 50% to
  60% and the equivalent 20% relative increase from the original baseline.
  Modeled estimates remain distinct from measured outcomes, a one-site result
  does not become universal, and association does not become causation.
- Authentic images can still mislead when attached to a different event. The
  page asks for the earliest available appearance, original caption, date,
  location, crop, and a match between what the image shows and what the current
  claim says.
- U.S. spoken guidance covers one-syllable uncountable `news`, stress in
  `headline`, `report`, and `reliable`, one-syllable `source`, the noun-verb
  stress shift in `record`, established U.S. pronunciations of `data`, linked
  `according to`, and contrastive stress on estimated versus measured.
- The five-item tutor-read check tests headline-body certainty, attribution,
  news countability, `record` stress, and modeled versus measured evidence
  before the learner relies on the written line.
- The fictional Clearwater file supplies an 80% decline for one packaged-food
  category at one cafeteria, a 9% decline in total waste at that site, one month
  of evidence, 24 city cafeterias, no citywide result, a published city method,
  and an independent researcher’s cautious recommendation. Every reading answer
  is recoverable without current-news knowledge.
- All 58 scored decisions have a named format, source role, exact claim,
  evidence type, status, stance, attribution relationship, numerical baseline,
  scope, listening line, reading fact, or exact repair target. Every decision
  has a contextual first hint, exact fix, and concept-specific correct feedback.
- Private-safe practice never requires a political belief, voting or browsing
  history, private message, social account, workplace, source contact, location,
  health or legal event, trauma, family conflict, or a real sharing mistake.
  Final production uses only the fictional files.
- The 90-second verification briefing requires product type, central claim,
  publisher, evidence, both percentages, scope correction, source-independence
  distinction, accurate headline, attributed summary, next check, and editor
  response. Delayed retrieval revisits countability, purpose, source-evidence
  role, unverified status, and percentage scope.

## Relationships: issues found and resolved

- The former lesson offered useful relationship and repair vocabulary but
  compressed a sensitive topic into thirteen scored decisions. The revision
  now contains 58 constrained decisions across labels, contact, support, trust,
  conflict, communication jobs, speech, listening, reading, building, repair,
  production, and delayed retrieval.
- The opening Cedar House conversation separates an observable missed update,
  its impact on Nora, a 3:00 request, Nora’s after-8:00 availability boundary,
  Eli’s paraphrase and responsibility, and three weeks of later follow-through.
  Trust is described as beginning to rebuild rather than instantly restored.
- Acquaintance, coworker, close friend, neighbor, and partner are defined by the
  relationship each word establishes and what it does not prove. Controlled
  items supply enough context to avoid guessing friendship, romance, or
  closeness.
- `Colleague` is allowed to cover the same workplace or a broader professional
  field. Gender-neutral `partner` remains useful, while romantic, business,
  project, or activity context is added when the type matters.
- The lesson explicitly rejects using eye contact, directness, affection, or
  message frequency as universal proof of closeness or respect. Relationship
  norms can vary across people, communities, and cultures.
- `Meet`, `get to know`, `get along`, `keep in touch`, `lose touch`, `grow
  apart`, and `drift apart` now encode separate contact and closeness changes.
  Getting along does not require complete agreement; growing apart does not
  automatically imply an argument.
- `Have something in common` names a shared interest, experience, background,
  or characteristic without automatically proving closeness.
- `Rely on`, `be there for`, `follow through`, `let someone down`, and `rebuild
  trust` are tied to supplied actions. Support does not require agreement with
  every choice, and trust can differ by domain rather than functioning as one
  permanent all-or-nothing label.
- A single apology may begin repair but does not guarantee restored trust.
  Repeated later behavior supplies the evidence for the limited trust claim in
  the Cedar House record.
- U.S. conflict language now stores complete relationships: `disagree with a
  person about an issue`, `argue with a person about an issue`, `have a
  falling-out with someone`, `apologize to a person for an action`, `discuss an
  issue`, and `talk an issue through`.
- `Had a falling-out` is prioritized as especially natural in U.S. English,
  while `fell out with` is acknowledged as understandable across English
  varieties. `Make up` is disambiguated from inventing, constituting, and the
  noun `makeup`.
- Apology, explanation, responsibility, forgiveness, restored trust,
  reconciliation, and continued contact remain separate outcomes. The page
  does not force forgiveness or claim that every conflict should be talked
  through or repaired.
- Observation, impact or feeling, need, request, boundary, and agreement are
  taught as different communication jobs. The page does not present one formula
  as a guarantee of a successful conversation.
- `I feel that you are selfish` is identified as a judgment despite its opening
  words. `Always` and `never` are not banned, but learners are warned against
  using them without evidence for every case.
- A request asks another person for a specific action they can accept, decline,
  or negotiate. A boundary states the speaker’s own availability, limit, and
  action. Calling a demand a boundary does not automatically make it respectful.
- U.S. spoken guidance covers stress in `acquaintance`, `supportive`,
  `boundary`, `apologize`, and `coworker`; linking in `know each other`, `rely
  on`, and `talk it through`; neutral particle prominence; and movement of
  sentence stress when information focus changes.
- The five-item tutor-read check tests growing apart without conflict,
  behavior-based support, the falling-out noun, request versus boundary, and
  contrastive setting stress before written support is used.
- The expanded Cedar House record supplies every time, commitment, missed
  action, stated feeling, request, boundary, apology, explanation, agreement,
  and later behavior needed for five defensible reading answers.
- All 58 scored decisions have a stated relationship type, contact history,
  support behavior, commitment, conflict structure, communication job,
  timeline, listening line, reading fact, or exact repair target. Every item
  has a contextual first hint, exact fix, and concept-specific correct feedback.
- Private-safe practice never requires a real family, friendship, romantic or
  workplace conflict, breakup, estrangement, betrayal, trauma, abuse, health
  condition, private message, identity, or contact decision. The page states
  that it teaches English rather than counseling and provides a fictional exit
  from every sensitive task.
- Final production requires the exact event, impact, need, request, boundary,
  paraphrase, supported responsibility, explanation without erased impact,
  apology, observable agreement, and later trust evidence. Delayed retrieval
  revisits label evidence, contact change, trust, U.S. conflict language, and
  request-boundary meaning.
