B2 extended reading · main idea

AI and the Work of Learning

Artificial intelligence is increasingly used in education to generate explanations, create practice activities, and give students immediate feedback. Supporters argue that these tools can make learning more flexible. A student who is reluctant to ask a question in class, for example, can request another explanation privately and without embarrassment. Teachers may also save time by using AI to produce first drafts of routine materials, leaving more time for discussion and individual support.

However, speed and convenience do not necessarily lead to deeper learning. When an answer appears instantly, students may accept it before considering why it is correct or whether it is reliable. This is particularly risky because AI systems can produce confident statements that are incomplete or inaccurate. If learners treat the technology as an authority rather than a tool, they may become less independent, not more.

The same concern applies to teachers. Automatically generated exercises may look polished while failing to address the specific misunderstandings of a particular class. Effective teaching requires noticing hesitation, asking follow-up questions, and adapting an explanation in response. These are judgments that technology can assist with, but not consistently replace.

For this reason, the most productive debate may not be whether AI belongs in education, but what role it should play. Used carefully, it can provide additional practice and reduce repetitive work. Used without clear goals, it may encourage superficial completion. Schools should therefore evaluate such tools according to the quality of thinking they produce, rather than the amount of content they generate.

7. Teacher-scored production

Teacher note: production evidence

Recognition can be stronger than spontaneous production. Use these tasks to confirm the recommended starting point, especially at B1 and B2.

Short personal interview

Teacher asks about name, country, work/study, daily routine, and likes.

Short written message

Write 5-7 sentences about a recent day, trip, meeting, or problem.

Opinion task

Speak for 2-3 minutes: Should people learn English for work, travel, or personal growth? Explain your opinion.

B1 connected writing

Write 100-140 words about a change at work, in your city, or in your daily life. Explain what changed, why it matters, and your opinion.

B2 discussion task

Discuss for 3-4 minutes: Should companies limit employees' use of AI at work? Consider benefits, risks, and reasonable limits.

Argument task

Write or outline a short argument: Is remote work better than office work? Include both sides and your conclusion.
