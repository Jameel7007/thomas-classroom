The Clearing Method

Mistakes aren't failures.
They're the lesson plan.

every error = a map of the student's first language
1

Notice

We talk about your real life — your job, your city, your plans. When an error appears, we don't skip past it. We catch the exact pattern, usually something your first language is quietly importing into English.

2

Clear

One small idea at a time, explained in seconds — not a grammar lecture. You rebuild the sentence yourself, step by step, so the fix comes from your own thinking, not from memorizing mine.

3

Drill

Then we make it automatic: quick, varied repetitions until the correct form is faster than the old mistake. You finish the lesson saying it right without thinking — which is the whole point of speaking.

Before you book

Questions I get
before lesson one.

Do I need to prepare anything?

No. Come as you are. Bring something real you'd like to talk about if you have it — work, travel, an interview — but I'll always have a plan if you don't.

What level do I need to start?

Anywhere from absolute beginner to advanced. If you can read this page, we can work together. Your first lesson maps where you actually are out loud — usually a level higher than people expect.

Is it grammar lessons or conversation?

Conversation first — but we stop and clear the grammar the moment it gets in the way. You get the rule in seconds, then we make it automatic. You can also browse the full A0–B2 curriculum map.

What if I freeze up or make mistakes?

That's the whole point — mistakes are the lesson plan. Lessons are built to feel safe, and reassurance comes first, because confidence is what unlocks speaking.

How long are lessons, and where?

50 minutes, one-on-one, online. I prepare materials around you and send notes after each session, so progress stays visible.
