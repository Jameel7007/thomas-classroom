Grammar · Lesson 02

Subject pronouns

Pronouns let us speak quickly without repeating names. Learners notice who is speaking, who is listening, and who we are talking about.

≈ 30 min I / you / he / she / it noticing + tile drill
NoticeLook at meaning before naming the rule.
DiscoverBuild the pattern from examples.
DrillRepeat with quick feedback.
UsePersonalise in short exchanges.

Notice the people

Read the mini-dialogue. Before explaining anything, point to the speaker, the listener, and the people being talked about.

Guided noticing

Ana: I am Ana. You are Leo. She is Maria. He is Tom. We are students.

  • Who says I?
  • Who is you?
  • Which words replace names?

The pattern

PronounMeaningQuick example
Ime, the speakerI am a student.
youthe listenerYou are my teacher.
heone male personHe is Tom.
sheone female personShe is Ana.
itone thing, animal, or ideaIt is a book.
weme + one or more peopleWe are classmates.
theytwo or more people/thingsThey are friends.

Teaching move

  • Use gestures first: point to yourself, the learner, one person, two people.
  • Name the grammar after the learner has already felt the pattern.
  • Contrast he / she / they with real classroom names.

Quick practice — choose the pronoun

1Ana → is from Mexico.
2Tom → is a student.
3A book → is blue.
4Ana and Tom → are friends.

Tile game — match the meaning

Click a pronoun tile, then click the matching meaning slot.

Communicate

Pair drill: point to people or objects in the room. Partner answers with a full sentence: She is Maria. It is a pencil. We are students.
Personalisation: learners write three true sentences using I, you, we, then read them aloud.
