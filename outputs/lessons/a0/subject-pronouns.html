Subject pronouns

Pronouns let us speak quickly without repeating names. Learners notice who is speaking, who is listening, and who we are talking about.

What is a pronoun?

A pronoun is a small word that takes the place of a name or a thing.

We use pronouns so we do not say the same name again and again.

Subject pronouns tell us who does the action, or who the sentence is about.

Before and after

  • Maria is a student. Maria is from Mexico.
    Maria is a student. She is from Mexico.  (She = Maria)
  • Tom is my friend. Tom is happy.
    Tom is my friend. He is happy.  (He = Tom)
  • The book is blue. The book is new.
    The book is blue. It is new.  (It = the book)

The second sentence is shorter and sounds more natural. The pronoun points back to the name.

The pattern

PronounMeaningQuick example
Ime, the speakerI am a student.
youthe listener — one or many peopleYou are my teacher.
heone male personHe is Tom.
sheone female personShe is Ana.
itone thing, animal, or ideaIt is a book.
weme + one or more peopleWe are classmates.
theytwo or more people, animals, or thingsThey are friends.

Teaching move

  • Use gestures first: point to yourself, the learner, one person, two people.
  • Name the grammar after the learner has already felt the pattern.
  • Contrast he / she / they with real classroom names.

Communicate

Pair drill: point to people or objects in the room. Partner answers with a full sentence: She is Maria. It is a pencil. We are students.
Personalization: learners write three true sentences using I, you, we, then read them aloud.

Reflect

Quick self-check

  • Can you say one true sentence about yourself with I?
  • Can you talk about a friend with he or she?
  • Can you talk about your family or team with we or they?
  • Do you use it for a thing, not he or she?
