Greetings & introductions

The first ten seconds of every meeting. Say hello, give your name, and reply politely. Good morning. My name is Ana. Nice to meet you. A few clean chunks open almost any conversation at work or online.

≈ 45–55 min hello / goodbye formal / informal 9 interactive tasks
NoticeRead a short first meeting.
DiscoverGreeting, name, reply, goodbye.
BuildMatch, choose, and build chunks.

We learn these as whole chunks, not single words: Nice to meet you · My name is… · How are you? · See you later. Say each one as one smooth phrase.

Notice before the rule

A first meeting at work

— Good morning. My name is Ana.   — Hi, Ana. I'm Daniel. Nice to meet you.   — Nice to meet you too. How are you?   — Fine, thanks. And you?

  • Which words do they use to greet each other at the start?
  • How does each person give a name? You can see two ways. What are they?
  • One person says Nice to meet you. How does the other person reply?

Discovery check — read the cue, then choose

The time in brackets tells you which greeting fits

1, everyone. Let's start the meeting.  (8 a.m.)
2. Thanks for the call.  (2 p.m.)
3. Welcome to the hotel.  (7 p.m.)
4. See you tomorrow.  (leaving)

The system: four moments

A short meeting has four quick moments. Learn one chunk for each:

1. Greet.  Informal: Hi · Hello.  By time of day: Good morning · Good afternoon · Good evening.

2. Give your name.  My name is Ana.  or the short way: I'm Ana.

3. Reply.  Nice to meet you. → Nice to meet you too.  How are you? → Fine, thanks. And you?

4. Leave.  Informal: Bye · See you later.  Neutral: Goodbye.

informal (a coworker, a friend)more formal (a client, your boss)

Meaning check

  • With a new client, is Hey, what's up? a good start? No. It is very informal. Use Good morning or Hello, and a last name: Mr. Lopez · Ms. Reyes.
  • Do we say I am name is Ana? No. Choose one: My name is Ana or I'm Ana.
  • Someone says How are you? What do you say? Reply, then ask back: Fine, thanks. And you?

Casual greetings with friends

With friends, English gets more relaxed. These all mean the same thing — a friendly Hi! How are you? — and the easy reply is almost always Not much. or Hey!

Two quick tips

  • What's up? is usually not a real question. Don't explain your whole day — just say Not much. You?
  • These are very informal. Great with friends and close coworkers, but with a new client or your boss, start with Hello or Good morning.

Pick the natural reply

Each line has a normal answer. Keep the pairs together:

Nice to meet you. → Nice to meet you too.
How are you? → Fine, thanks. And you?

Drill 1 — choose the best reply

The cue in brackets tells you the situation

1— Nice to meet you.  — . (reply to a new client)
2— How are you?  — . And you? (reply on a call)
3— Hi, I'm Daniel.  — . I'm Ana. (greet back, informal)
4— See you tomorrow.  — . Have a good day. (end of the meeting)

Bye! See you later.

Use it in real life

Meet a coworker: It is your first day. Greet a new coworker, give your name, and reply to theirs. Hi, I'm…  Nice to meet you.  How are you?
On a video call: The call starts at 9 a.m. Greet the group and say your name clearly. Good morning, everyone. My name is…
Introduce a colleague: Introduce a third person to the group. This is my colleague, Daniel. Daniel, this is Ms. Reyes.
On the phone: Answer and start a call politely. Hello, this is Ana.  Good afternoon. How are you?
End and leave: Close the conversation two ways — with a coworker, then with a client. Bye! See you later.  Goodbye. Have a good day.

Reflect

Run the four moments

Say a full first meeting aloud, in order: “______ (greet). My name is ______. ______ (reply to a name). ______ (ask how someone is). ______ (leave).”

Then do it twice: once informal with a coworker, once more formal with a client. Notice which words you change.
