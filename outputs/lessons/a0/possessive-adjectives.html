Possessive adjectives

One small word shows who owns something: my phone, your email, her office, their car. Each subject pronoun has a partner — I → my, she → her, they → their — and it always sits before the noun.

≈ 45–55 min who owns it? before the noun 9 interactive tasks

A possessive adjective never changes for one thing or many: my book / my books, her client / her clients. The owner decides the word, not the thing owned.

Notice before the rule

Who owns it?

I have a phone.  →  This is my phone.
She has an office.  →  This is her office.
They have a car.  →  This is their car.

  • One word changed in each pair: I → my, she → her, they → their. What job does the new word do?
  • Where does it sit — before the noun, or after it?
  • Does the word for "phone, office, car" change? What stays the same?

Discovery check — read who owns it, then choose

The system: one partner for each pronoun

Every subject pronoun has one possessive partner. Learn them as pairs:

Meaning check

  • Does the possessive go before or after the noun? Before: my manager, never manager my.
  • Is it his or her? Look at the owner, not the thing. Daniel's office is his office; Elena's car is her car.
  • Does the word change for one or many? No. my book and my books both use my.

From pronoun to possessive

Keep the pairs together. Say each one aloud: I — my, you — your, he — his, she — her, we — our, they — their.

Use it in real life

Your desk: Name four things at your desk with my. This is my phone. These are my notes. That is my coffee.
Your team: Tell me about three coworkers. Use his and her for their jobs and things. Daniel is on his client call. Elena is in her office. This is her email.
Your family: Describe two family members and what they own. My sister is a nurse. Her car is small. My parents have a dog. Their dog is friendly.
Our place: Describe your workplace or home with our. This is our office. Our team is small. Our meeting room is upstairs.
Whose is it? (info gap): The teacher names a person; the student answers with the possessive. Teacher: Daniel, keys. → Student: These are his keys. Continue with new people and things.
