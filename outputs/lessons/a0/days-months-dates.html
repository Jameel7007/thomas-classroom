Days, months & dates

Run your week and your calendar in English. Name the seven days, the twelve months, and say a date the American way: March 8th. Then book meetings, deadlines, and appointments out loud.

Days and months always start with a capital letter in English: Monday, Tuesday, January, March — never monday or march.

Notice before the rule

Read the calendar

Meeting — on Monday   ·   Deadline — on March 8th   ·   Vacation — in July   ·   New office — in 2026

  • Two entries use on and two use in. Which words come after on? Which words come after in?
  • One is an exact day. One is an exact date. One is a whole month. One is a whole year. Which is the biggest block of time?
  • So which little word goes with an exact day or date, and which goes with a long block like a month or year?

The whole month is March → in March. One exact date is March 8th (circled) → on March 8th. Say it month-first: March eighth.

The system: days, months, dates

Learn the words as a set, then add the little words for time.

Seven days:  Monday · Tuesday · Wednesday · Thursday · Friday · Saturday · Sunday. The work week starts on Monday.

Twelve months:  January · February · March · April · May · June · July · August · September · October · November · December.

Saying the date, American style

  • We say the month first, then the day: March 8th. We say it as an ordinal: March eighth.
  • Ordinals count position: first (1st), second (2nd), third (3rd), fourth (4th), fifth (5th)… thirty-first (31st).
  • To ask the date: What's the date today?  Answer: It's June 16th.
  • Can we say in Monday? No. A day is exact, so use on Monday.

Use it in real life

Your weekly schedule: Say one thing you do each workday. On Monday I have a team meeting. On Friday I finish early.
Your birthday: Tell the teacher your birthday and your month. My birthday is on June 16th. It's in June.
Book an appointment: Role-play with the teacher. I'd like an appointment on Wednesday.  Is March 8th okay?
A deadline: Pick a real or invented deadline and say it. The report is due on Thursday.  The project ends in December.
What's the date? Take turns. One person asks What's the date today? The other answers with a full date, then asks back.

Reflect

Explain the choice

Complete this aloud: “I use on for ______ (a day or a date). I use in for ______ (a month or a year). I say the date with the month first, like ______.”

Then say three true sentences about your week: one with on + a day, one with in + a month, and one date in American style.
