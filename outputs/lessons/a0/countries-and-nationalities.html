Countries & nationalities

On a video call, someone asks: Where are you from? You answer with a country — I'm from Brazil — or with a nationality — I'm Brazilian. This is the last lesson of A0, so you will use language you already know to talk about real people: you, your coworkers, your clients, your friends.

Notice before the rule

Two ways to answer one question

I'm from Brazil.  →  I'm Brazilian.
I'm from Japan.  →  I'm Japanese.

  • Which word names the country (the place)? Which word is the nationality (the person)?
  • After from, do we use the country or the nationality? And after am / is / are?
  • Look at the endings: Brazil → Brazilian, Japan → Japanese. The nationality is built from the country. What changed?

Discovery check — from or be?

After from use the country. After am / is / are use the nationality.

2I'm .  (after am → nationality)
3She's .  (after is → nationality)
4They're from .  (after from → country)

The system: from a country, be a nationality

There are two simple patterns. Keep these chunks together:

1. from + country.  Where are you from?  →  I'm from Spain.

2. be + nationality.  I'm Spanish.  She's Spanish.  They're Spanish.

The nationality is an adjective, built from the country with a common ending. Group them and the spelling gets easy:

endingcountrynationality
-ianBrazil · Italy · UkraineBrazilian · Italian · Ukrainian
-anMexico · Germany · the United StatesMexican · German · American
-ishTurkey · Spain · PolandTurkish · Spanish · Polish
-eseJapan · ChinaJapanese · Chinese
specialFrance · ColombiaFrench · Colombian

Meaning check

  • Can we say I'm from Brazilian? No. After from we name the country: I'm from Brazil.
  • Can we say I'm Brazil? No. After be we use the nationality: I'm Brazilian.
  • Do we write spanish with a small letter? No. Countries and nationalities always take a capital: Spain, Spanish.
  • Is the nationality the same for he, she, and they? Yes. The adjective never changes: He's French. She's French. They're French.

Use it in real life

About you: Answer the question two ways. Where are you from? — I'm from…  I'm… Use your real country and nationality.
Your coworkers: Think of two people you work with. Say where each one is from. My coworker Sofia is from Mexico. She's Mexican.
Your clients: Name a client or company abroad. Our client is from Germany. He's German.  Then ask the teacher: Where is your client from?
On a video call: Role-play meeting someone new online. Hi, I'm Lena. Where are you from? — I'm from Poland. And you?
Friends and travel: Talk about a friend from another country, or a country you want to visit. My friend is Italian. I want to go to Japan.
