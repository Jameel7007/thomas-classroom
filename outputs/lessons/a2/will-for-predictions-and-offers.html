The system: will and won’t

Will works like can. It is the same word for every subject, the next verb stays in the base form, and questions and negatives are made without do.

formpatternexample
positivesubject + will + base verbI will be there. She will call you tonight.
negativesubject + won’t + base verbIt won’t take long. They won’t agree.
questionWill + subject + base verbWill you be home at six?
short answerwill / won’tYes, I will. No, she won’t.

No -s and no do: say she will call, not she wills call, and Will you help?, not Do you will help?

Contractions make it sound natural

In speech, will contracts to ’ll after pronouns: I’ll, you’ll, he’ll, she’ll, it’ll, we’ll, they’ll. Will not almost always becomes won’t. Short answers keep the full word: say Yes, I will, never Yes, I’ll.

Get to the root: the moment of speaking

With will, the future is not planned in advance. You form the idea now, while you are talking: you believe something, you volunteer, you commit yourself, or you choose. Four everyday uses share this one root.

useexamplewhat is happening
predictionI think it’ll rain tomorrow.You say what you believe about the future.
instant decisionI’ll take this one.You decide now, while you are speaking.
offerI’ll carry that for you.You volunteer help at this moment.
promiseI won’t be late.You commit yourself to a future action.

Why will does the grammar work

Will is a modal, from the same auxiliary family as can. It agrees with nothing, moves to the front for questions, and combines directly with not. The main verb never changes.

Follow the moving part

She will call you. → Will she call you? → She won’t call you.

Only will moves or becomes negative. Call stays in the base form in all three sentences. This is why Does she will call? mixes two different systems and is not the target form.

Will and be going to

Both talk about the future, so the choice depends on two questions: when did you decide, and what is your prediction based on?

your situationchooseexample
decided before now (a plan)be going toWe’re going to visit my parents on Saturday. We decided last week.
decided right now, while speakingwill"There’s no bread." "OK, I’ll get some on my way home."
prediction from evidence you can seebe going toLook at those clouds. It’s going to rain.
prediction from opinion or experiencewillI think it’ll rain tomorrow.

The classic A2 mix-up

"I’m going to make pasta tonight. I bought everything yesterday." "Nice. Then I’ll bring a salad."

The first speaker planned dinner before this conversation, so she uses going to. The second speaker decides during the conversation, so he uses will.

  • When did you decide? Before now: going to. Right now: will.
  • What is the prediction based on? Evidence in front of you: going to. Your opinion or experience: will.

Softeners: I think, probably, maybe

Predictions with will usually sound better with a softener, because you are giving an opinion, not a fact: I think they’ll accept the offer, She’ll probably agree, Maybe I’ll stay home this weekend.

Word order: probably goes after will but before won’t. Say She’ll probably agree and He probably won’t come.

Use it in real life

Three predictions: Say three sentences about your next month with I think … will. I think my team will finish the project. I think the summer will be hot.
Offer help: The teacher describes small problems: a heavy suitcase, a difficult email, an empty coffee pot. Answer each one with an offer. I’ll carry it. I’ll check it for you. I’ll make more.
Make promises: Say three promises to a friend, your boss, or your teacher with will or won’t. I’ll send the report tonight. I won’t forget our meeting.
Decide right now: You are in a café and the teacher reads you three menu options. Decide out loud in the moment. I’ll have the soup and a coffee.
Ten years from now: Make predictions about your city, your work, or your English with probably and maybe. My city will probably be bigger. Maybe I’ll live near the sea.
