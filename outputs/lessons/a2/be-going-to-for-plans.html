Future plans with be going to

Talk about plans you have already decided and predictions you can already see coming: I’m going to visit my parents in June, She isn’t going to take the job, and Look at those clouds. It’s going to rain.

Be going to is the everyday future for things you have already decided and for things you can already see starting to happen.

Notice before the rule

Two futures, one pattern

"I’m going to visit my parents in June. I bought the tickets last week."
"Look at those clouds. It’s going to rain."

  • Which sentence is about a plan the speaker decided before speaking?
  • Which sentence is a prediction? What can the speaker see right now?
  • Both sentences share the same three-part chunk. Find it: a form of be, then going to, then a verb.

Discovery check: choose the pattern

Use the subject to choose the form of be. The rest of the chunk never changes.

1I going to start a new job in May.
2She going to call you tonight.
3We going to rent a bigger apartment.
4He going to drive; he wants to walk.
5They going to stay long; they leave at six.
6 you going to watch the game tonight?

The system: am / is / are + going to + base verb

The chunk has three parts. Only the first part, the verb be, changes with the subject. Going to and the base verb stay the same for everyone.

Ihe / she / ityou / we / they
positiveI’m going to call him.She’s going to call him.We’re going to call him.
negativeI’m not going to call him.She isn’t going to call him.We aren’t going to call him.
questionAm I going to be late?Is she going to call him?Are you going to call him?

Be is the moving part. It agrees with the subject, moves to the front in questions, and carries not in negatives: Are you going to…? She isn’t going to… The main verb after going to is always the base form.

Get to the root: a future you can already see

Why one chunk for two ideas? In both uses, the future is already in motion now. A plan exists in your head before you speak. A prediction grows out of evidence in front of your eyes. You are “going toward” something that has already started.

useexamplewhere the future comes from
plan decided before speakingI’m going to visit my parents in June.The decision already exists. You are reporting it, not making it now.
prediction from present evidenceLook at those clouds. It’s going to rain.Something you can see, hear, or know now points to the result.

Test the meaning

"She’s going to study nursing." A decision she made before this conversation.

"It’s 5:58 and the train leaves at 6:00. We’re going to miss it." No decision at all, just clear evidence in front of you. Same chunk, two roots, one idea: the future is already visible now.

Questions, short answers, and fast speech

For a question, move the be verb in front of the subject. Short answers use only the be verb; never repeat going to.

questionshort answer, yesshort answer, no
Are you going to drive?Yes, I am.No, I’m not.
Is she going to take the job?Yes, she is.No, she isn’t.
Are they going to move?Yes, they are.No, they aren’t.

What you hear: “gonna”

In fast speech, going to often sounds like gonna: “I’m gonna call you later.” Learn to recognize it when you listen. In writing and careful speech, use going to.

Going to go

I’m going to go to the bank is correct, but speakers often shorten it to I’m going to the bank. In the short version, going is the main verb, and the sentence still points to the future.

Use it in real life

Three real plans: Say three plans you have already decided for this month. Use times or dates if you can. I’m going to renew my passport next week.
Ask the teacher: Ask three Are you going to…? questions about the weekend. Listen and give one follow-up question. Are you going to cook? Are you going to see friends?
Someone you know: Describe another person’s real plans with he’s going to or she’s going to. My coworker is going to change teams in July.
Evidence predictions: Make two predictions from things you can see or know right now. It’s 11:55, so the meeting is going to end soon. My phone is at two percent. It’s going to die.
Not going to: Say three things you aren’t going to do this year, and why. I’m not going to change apartments. I like my street.
