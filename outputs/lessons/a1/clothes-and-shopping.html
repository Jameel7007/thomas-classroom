Vocabulary · Lesson 17

Clothes & shopping

Name the clothes you wear and shop with confidence. The key trick: one item takes is (this shirt is…), and items that come in a pair or as "many" take are (these shoes are…). Then add the shopping phrases — How much is this?, Can I try it on?, I'll take it.

A few clothes are always plural because they have two legs or two sides: pants, jeans, shorts, glasses. We count them as a pair of jeans, two pairs of pants. (British English says trousers for pants — we use the American word here.)

Notice before the rule

In a clothing store

"Excuse me, how much is this shirt? … And how much are these shoes?"

  • One question uses is and the other uses are. Why? Look at shirt and shoes.
  • A shirt is one item. Shoes come as a pair — two of them. Which word, is or are, goes with "many"?
  • So before you choose is or are, ask one question. What is it?

The system: one item or a pair?

Before you choose this / these and is / are, ask one quick question:

One item or more than one?  One → this shirt is…  More than one (or a pair) → these shoes are…

Meaning check

  • Can we say these shoe? No. These means many, so the noun is plural: these shoes.
  • Why is it these pants are… and not this pant is…? Pants is always plural (two legs). The same is true for jeans, shorts, glasses.
  • How do we count an always-plural item? Use a pair of: a pair of jeans, two pairs of sneakers.

Shopping phrases to keep whole

Learn these as fixed chunks. Match is / are to the item, then keep the rest the same:

How much is this…?   ·   How much are these…?
What size is it?   ·   Can I try it on?   ·   It fits. / It doesn't fit.   ·   I'll take it.   ·   It's on sale.

Sizes are simple at A1: small, medium, large. Prices use the dollar sign: $25, $40.

Read and choose

A message from the store

“Hi! I’m at the store. The blue jeans are $40 and this black coat is $60. They don’t have your size in the white sneakers. Do you want the gray t-shirt? It’s only $12.”

Read, then choose

1. How much is the coat?

2. What don’t they have?

3. What costs $12?

Use it in real life

At the store (role play): One person is the customer, one is the salesperson. Use the chunks: How much is this?  What size?  Can I try it on?  It fits.  I'll take it.
What are you wearing? Describe your clothes right now in full sentences. This is a gray sweater. These are black sneakers. My jacket is blue.
Clothes for work: Say what you wear to work or to a meeting. I wear a suit and a white shirt. I wear comfortable shoes.
Packing for travel: You travel for three days. Name four clothes you pack. I pack two shirts, a pair of jeans, a jacket, and sneakers.
On sale: The teacher names a price and an item; the student reacts. These boots are $40. That's a good price — I'll take them!

Reflect

Explain the choice

Complete this aloud: “For one item, I say this ___ is…. For a pair, I say these ___ are…. Three clothes that are always plural are ______, ______, and ______. To ask the price of one thing I say ______; for a pair I say ______.”

Then say one true sentence about something you are wearing now, and one shopping question you could ask in a store.
