Discovery check: countable or uncountable?

The system

sentenceusual wordexample
positivesomeThere are some apples. We have some rice.
negativeanyThere aren’t any apples. We don’t have any rice.
questionanyAre there any apples? Is there any rice?

Countable nouns have singular/plural forms: a cup / two cups. Uncountable nouns normally do not: water, rice, information. Use a container when needed: two bottles of water.

Get to the root: English chooses a counting lens

Countability is not only about the real object. It is about how English chooses to view the noun in this meaning. A countable noun is packaged as separate units; an uncountable noun is viewed as a mass, substance, or general idea without natural unit boundaries.

Count the container or piece

To count an uncountable idea, add a unit: two cups of coffee, three pieces of information, a bottle of water, two items of furniture. The container or piece becomes countable; the substance noun stays unchanged.

Some and any do not mean an exact number

Both words point to an indefinite quantity: the exact amount or identity is not important. The difference is often the speaker’s orientation.

wordspeaker’s orientationexample
somepresents a quantity as existing or expectedWe have some coffee.
anyleaves existence open, or says zero in a negativeDo we have any coffee? We don’t have any.

This explains the beginner pattern better than “some is positive, any is negative.” That shortcut is useful, but the speaker’s expectation explains important exceptions.

Offers and requests: why questions can use some

Use some in a question when you are offering something or requesting a quantity you expect can be provided.

questionspeaker’s idea
Would you like some coffee?I am offering coffee that I believe is available.
Can I have some water?I am requesting a normal amount, not asking whether water exists anywhere.
Is there any coffee?I genuinely do not know whether coffee is available.

Common noun traps

In standard English, these are normally uncountable: advice, information, furniture, luggage, homework, work, traffic. Say some advice or a piece of advice, not an advice. Some nouns change countability with meaning: coffee is a substance, but two coffees can mean two cups or orders in a café.
