Prepositions of time and place

Place every plan and every object precisely: at 9:30, on Monday, in July, at work, on the table, and in the kitchen.

At, on, and in are the small words that put times on a clock, days on a calendar, and things in places. You choose by size: a point, a day or surface, or a period or space.

The system: time with at, on, and in

For time, choose by the size of the period. The smaller and more exact the time, the more likely it is at.

Small, medium, large

The review is at 9:30 on Monday in July.

One sentence, three sizes: at 9:30 is a point on the clock, on Monday is one square on the calendar, and in July is a whole month around it. Zoom out, and the preposition changes.

Three classic exceptions

Memorize these as chunks: at night, on the weekend (American English), and in the morning / in the afternoon / in the evening. Notice the pattern shift: in the morning, but on Friday morning, because the named day becomes the organizing unit.

No preposition before this, next, last, every: this morning, next Friday, last year, every Monday.

The system: place with at, on, and in

For place, ask what picture you see: a point you stop at, a surface or line something sits on, or a space with an inside. With activities and institutions, English treats the location as a functional point: at work, at school, at a meeting. The focus is being there for the activity, not standing inside one exact room.

Zoom in, and the preposition changes

The conference is in Mexico, in Guadalajara, at the convention center, in Room 4, at the front.

There is no contradiction. Each phrase changes the scale of the mental map, from a large container down to a precise point.

Get to the root: point, surface, container

The same three pictures organize both the map and the calendar. At marks a point, on marks a surface or a line (a street, a floor, a row of days), and in marks something with an inside (a room, a city, a month). When two prepositions are both possible, they paint different pictures.

sentencepicture
She is at the hospital.Her general location or purpose is the hospital.
She is in the hospital.She is inside; in American English this often means she is a patient.
He is at the table.He is beside it, sitting or working there.
The keys are on the table.They touch its surface.

Prepositions do not translate word for word. Learn combinations as chunks: at work, on time, in bed, on the weekend.

Use it in real life

Your real week: Say five true sentences about your schedule with at, on, and in. I work on Saturdays. My class starts at 7:00. My vacation is in August.
Home tour: Say where five real things are in your home. The coffee is in the cabinet. The remote is on the sofa.
Your address: Describe where you live, from small to large: your floor, your street, your city, your country. I live on the fourth floor, on Reforma Street, in Guadalajara, in Mexico.
Ask the teacher: Ask five When or Where questions and listen for at, on, and in in the answers. When is your next class? Where are your keys right now?
A typical Monday: Walk through your Monday with times. At 6:30 I get up. At noon I have lunch. In the evening I rest.
