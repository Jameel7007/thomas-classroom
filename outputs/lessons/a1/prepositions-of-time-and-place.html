Think in chunks

Learn useful combinations together: at work, on time, in bed, on the weekend (American English).

Get to the root: point, surface, container

Prepositions express how the speaker mentally organizes a relationship. English often uses the same three spatial pictures for both place and time.

pictureplacetime
at = a pointat the entrance
The entrance is a point on a route.		at 9:15
9:15 is a precise point on a timeline.
on = a surface or lineon the table
The object touches a surface.		on Tuesday
Tuesday is one named space on a calendar line.
in = inside boundariesin the office
The office contains the person.		in July
July is a time container with many days inside it.

Zoom in and the preposition can change

The meeting is in Mexico, in Guadalajara, at the convention center, in Room 4, at the front.

There is no contradiction. Each phrase changes the scale of the mental map—from a large container to a precise point.

Notice how extra detail changes the choice: in the morning, but on Friday morning because the named day becomes the organizing unit.

No preposition with these time words

Do not normally use at/on/in before this, next, last, every: this morning, next Friday, last year, every Monday.

Place: physical picture and social function

With physical objects, the point–surface–container picture is strong: at the door, on the shelf, in the drawer. With institutions and activities, English often treats the location as a functional point: at work, at school, at the airport. The focus is being present at that activity or location, not necessarily being inside a particular room.

contrastmeaning
She is at the hospital.Her general location or purpose is the hospital.
She is in the hospital.She is inside; in American English this can also suggest she is a patient.
He is at the table.He is beside it, normally sitting or working there.
The keys are on the table.They touch its surface.

Prepositions are not translated word-for-word reliably. Learn common combinations as meaningful chunks, then connect new examples to the point–surface–container picture.
