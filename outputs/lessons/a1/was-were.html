Get to the root: move a state onto a past timeline

Was/were is the past form of be. It connects a person or thing to a description, identity, age, condition, or location at an earlier time. It does not normally describe an action; it tells us what the situation was.

past connectionexampleinformation
locationWe were in Oaxaca.where we were
conditionI was tired.how I felt
descriptionThe hotel was quiet.what it was like
identity / roleShe was my manager.who she was then
ageHe was twenty.his age then

Present and past are parallel systems

I am busy today → I was busy yesterday.
They are at home now → They were at home last night.

The meaning relationship stays the same; only the time location changes.

Why there is no did

Be is unusual because it makes its own negatives and questions. It does not need the helper do/did. Move was/were before the subject for a question; attach not for a negative.

statementquestionnegative
She was ready.Was she ready?She wasn’t ready.
They were outside.Were they outside?They weren’t outside.

Compare an action verb: Did she work? But with be: Was she at work? Never combine them as Did she was…?

Time reference: make “past” clear

A past-time expression often tells the listener where to place the situation: yesterday, last night, in 2024, two hours ago, when I was a child. Once the past time is clear from context, you do not need to repeat it in every sentence.

A small past scene

The meeting was on Tuesday. It was at a hotel downtown. The room was small, but the chairs were comfortable. About twenty people were there.

After the first sentence establishes Tuesday, every following was/were naturally belongs to that same past scene.

There was / there were

Combine this lesson with there is/there are to say what existed in a past scene: There was a problem (one thing) and There were two empty seats (plural). Questions follow the same movement: Was there a problem? Were there any seats?

Born uses was/were

English says I was born in 1990 and They were born in Brazil. Treat was/were born as a useful fixed chunk. Ask Where were you born?, not Where did you born?
