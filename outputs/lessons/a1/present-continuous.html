Get to the root: an action viewed from inside

The present continuous does more than say “now.” It presents an action as open and in progress. Imagine a video with a beginning and an end. The speaker places the camera somewhere between those two points. At that camera moment, the action is not finished.

The mental picture

She is writing a report.

The sentence does not tell us when she started or when she will finish. It tells us that the activity is open around the time we are talking about. She may pause for coffee and still be “writing a report” as her current project.

  • Has the report-writing started? Yes.
  • Is it presented as finished? No.
  • Must her pen be moving at this exact second? Not always. It can be a temporary activity around now.

Why there are two verbs

English builds this meaning in two parts. The verb be carries the subject and tense: I am, she is, they are. The -ing verb names the unfolding activity. That is why She working is incomplete: the sentence has an activity, but no finite verb to anchor it.

partjobexample
am / is / areagrees with the subject; makes negatives and questionsIs she working? She isn’t working.
verb-ingshows the activity as unfoldingShe is working.

Present simple or present continuous?

The difference is usually not the action itself. It is the speaker’s viewpoint. The present simple looks at a stable pattern from the outside; the present continuous looks at an open situation from the inside.

stable patternopen situation
I work at home.
That is my normal arrangement.		I’m working at home today.
This is the temporary situation now.
She takes the bus.
That is her routine.		She’s taking a taxi.
That is happening on this occasion.
What do you do?
What is your job or routine?		What are you doing?
What activity is in progress?

Time expressions are clues, not automatic rules. Every day, usually often support the present simple; now, today, at the moment often support the continuous. First understand the meaning, then use the clue.

Spelling is connected to sound

We add -ing to the base verb, but spelling sometimes protects the original sound. Drop a final silent e (make → making). Double a final consonant after one short stressed vowel (sit → sitting, run → running). Change ie to y (lie → lying). Most verbs simply add -ing.

One useful limit

Some verbs normally describe a state, not an unfolding action: know, understand, want, need, like. At this level, prefer I understand and I need help, not I’m understanding or I’m needing help.
